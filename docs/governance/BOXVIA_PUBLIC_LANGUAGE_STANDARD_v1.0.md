# BoxVia Public Language Standard v1.0
Date: 2026-11-18
Status: APPROVED by project owner
Scope: All public BoxVia website pages and associated public-facing commercial copy.
Production: No merge or release without explicit owner approval.

## Governing principle
Write for the visitor's question and the deliverable, not for the internal methodology. A visitor should understand the offer within 10-15 seconds. Use short, plain, precise English and concrete outcomes. Avoid jargon, overclaiming, and unverified guarantees. Mandatory presentation rule: each complete sentence starts on a new line in rendered public-facing text, including headings, lead copy, cards, and calls to action. Prefer separate paragraphs for prose and explicit line breaks within multi-sentence headings or slogans. Do not rely on source-code newlines, which HTML collapses. Preserve semantic HTML and accessible reading order. Legal, privacy, contractual, and normative texts may retain conventional paragraphing where sentence-by-sentence layout would harm clarity or legal accuracy. Do not split abbreviations, version identifiers, monetary figures, URLs, or formal normative labels. Do not use em dashes or en dashes in public text.

## Audience and architecture
Four equal navigation audiences: Business & Consulting; Developers & Engineers; AI Builders & Creators; Researchers & Architects.
One shared service catalog. No service permanently dominates the homepage; campaign priority belongs on relevant audience/service landing pages.
BoxVia assessments may cover project definition and non-AI objects, not just AI systems.
Public website language is English only. Working and discussion language is Russian. When English copy is drafted or reviewed in chat, provide an accurate Russian technical translation when needed to support approval. Do not publish Russian translations on the English-only site.
Normative ANDEKS documents have their own stricter RU-to-EN technical translation rules. The site policy does not replace those rules.
Russian editorial copy must not use the letter ё. No long dashes in either language.

## Public service naming
Use **Project Definition Review** as the public name of the service previously shown as FAAR Stage 1.
Keep **FAAR Stage 1** as the methodology/protocol identifier in technical documentation, reports and, where necessary, a secondary note on the service page.
Do not imply that Project Definition Review guarantees feasibility, regulatory approval or launch readiness.
Proposed concise public description:
"Define what your project involves, what needs to be assessed, and what must be clarified before moving forward."
Proposed outcome description, subject to actual service scope:
"A written project map, key dependencies, identified gaps and recommended next steps."
Existing published starting price remains unchanged unless separately approved.
Do not rename the other services merely by analogy. Review each against its actual scope before changing its public name.

## Language levels
1. Home and four audience pages: simple, outcome-first English; minimal terminology.
2. Service pages: clear scope, deliverables, inputs, price/quote, boundaries and limitations; technical terms only when needed for accuracy.
3. Research, ANDEKS and architect-specific materials: precise specialist terminology is permitted.

## Plain-language rendering, not terminology replacement
- evidence boundary: "what the evidence can show" in public explanation.
- assessment object: "what we are assessing" in public explanation.
- UNESTABLISHED: "not enough evidence to reach a conclusion" in explanatory copy; retain UNESTABLISHED in formal findings when required.
- normative assessment: "independent assessment" only where the broader phrase is not misleading.
- pre-engagement regulatory architecture: explain in plain language; do not represent the simplified phrase as a formal definition.
- AI Governance Gap Assessment: consider an accessible descriptor; do not automatically conflate it with Focused AI Governance Review.
Simplifications must preserve evidence limits, methods, contractual scope and factual status.

## Human accountability and evidence
"AI-assisted. Human-assessed. Evidence-based." is approved positioning.
Explain the human-review procedure without asserting that every past or current case passed it. Distinguish observed evidence, interpretation, and what cannot be established.
No certification, legal approval, guaranteed outcome or substitute for the object owner's decision.
Sample reports must be distinguished from real paid client engagements.

## Editorial acceptance checklist
For every page: identify target audience, visitor question, offered result, next action, factual scope and limits; verify link targets, existing forms, legal/privacy language, canonical and SEO metadata; eliminate unexplained jargon, long dashes, redundant copy and misleading promises; verify English meaning against the approved Russian working formulation where needed.
Run static and Preview tests, record PASS/RETEST/HOLD/UNESTABLISHED. Do not change main or Production without owner approval.
