When Stage 1 helps

Your request spans several companies, activities or jurisdictions.

Roles, operational flows or dependencies are not yet clear.

A list of requested services exists, but there is not enough information to define a reliable assignment.

What we examine

We reconstruct the relevant participants, roles, operations, flows, boundaries and dependencies from the agreed materials.

For regulatory projects, this may include licensing, facilities, goods, import and customs models, ownership transitions and authority touchpoints.

Confirmed elements are separated from assumptions and unresolved questions.

What you receive

Project Architecture Summary

A concise account of the project structure established within the review boundary.

Entity / Role / Flow Map

A map of participants, functions, flows and important dependencies.

Preliminary Workstream Map

A distinction between supported, conditional and unresolved areas of future work.

Blind-Spot Register

A record of critical gaps, missing inputs and unresolved assumptions.

Stage-2 Engagement Frame

A basis for defining subsequent scope, sequencing, assumptions and exclusions.

The agreed depth depends on the project boundary and available evidence.

Listing gaps alone is not the endpoint: the report explains what those gaps mean for the next stage.

If the evidence remains insufficient, the report identifies what must be resolved before reliable downstream scoping is possible.

About FAAR

FAAR stands for Formal Architecture Assessment Review.

It is a methodology for examining a defined architecture using evidence and preserving the limits of the resulting conclusions.

Stage 1 is a pre-engagement application of FAAR, illustrated here through a regulatory project.

The public demonstrator is English Derivative v2.0, published on 6 July 2026.

The separately maintained FAAR methodological core is v1.0.

Stage 1 does not provide a final legal opinion, customs or controlled-goods classification, filing service or approval guarantee.

It provides a basis for scoping subsequent work, rather than a ready-to-execute implementation plan.

FAAR is not itself an ANDEKS normative assessment and does not imply ARE or ERA processing.
